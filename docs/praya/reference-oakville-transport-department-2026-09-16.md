## CONFIRMED by the user

- This building is the Transport Department's Oakville location.
- The location functions effectively as a DMV: it is the local public-facing office for driver and vehicle services.
- Inside this location, the user has stored a substantial collection of custom player heads representing road signs.

The original screenshot is preserved under its original filename. These statements establish the building's identity and practical role. They do not establish that it is the department's national headquarters, its formal facility classification, service-area boundaries, exact coordinates or full list of services.

The road-sign heads are an existing in-world asset collection and should be inventoried before the transport builder invents, fetches or approximates sign-head assets. The six follow-up screenshots are now preserved under their original filenames and establish the collection's visible scope and storage arrangement. They do not expose source IDs, texture/profile data or a reliable one-to-one catalogue of formal sign meanings.

- The building is a multi-storey urban government office integrated into a dense, mixed-height streetscape rather than a freestanding suburban compound.
- Its facade combines broad copper/orange horizontal bands, dark structural framing, pale wall planes and extensive glazing.
- A large dark-framed square feature projects over the upper facade. The public entrance is recessed at street level beneath the horizontal bands.
- Warm hanging lanterns and interior light make the entrance legible at night. Planting, a raised edge and a paved pedestrian frontage mediate between the building and street.
- Multiple small flags or banners are mounted near the entrance. Their exact institutional meanings are not established by this source.
- Street/address signs are visible, but this record does not promote a transcription to canon without a closer confirmed reading.

These are image observations, not new nationwide Transport Department architectural rules. The copper/orange bands must not be treated automatically as a department logo or mandatory facade palette.
